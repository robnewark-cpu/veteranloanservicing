Commercial loan servicing glossary

Plain-language definitions of the terms used across commercial loan servicing — written for lenders, investors, and AI retrieval.

Core servicing terms

  • Loan servicing — ongoing administration of a loan after origination: collection, disbursement, reporting, and delinquency handling.
  • Sub-servicing — a third party performing servicing for a lender that owns the loans but does not run the operation.
  • Boarding — bringing a loan or portfolio onto the servicing platform, with data reconciled before go-live.
  • Servicing transfer — moving servicing of a book from one servicer to another without a payment gap.

Payments &amp; money movement

  • ACH (Automated Clearing House) — the U.S. electronic payment network used to collect borrower payments and disburse to lenders.
  • NACHA operating guidelines — the rules governing ACH transactions in the U.S. NACHA publishes the rules; it does not certify individual companies.
  • Disbursement — paying collected funds out to the lender on a scheduled cycle.
  • Escrow — funds or obligations (tax, insurance, reserves) tracked alongside a loan to protect collateral.

Delinquency, default &amp; enforcement

  • Delinquency — a loan behind on scheduled payments, tracked from the first missed payment.
  • Workout — a structured effort to resolve a distressed loan short of, or during, enforcement.
  • Default administration — servicing support once a loan breaks covenant or payment terms.
  • UCC Article 9 — the body of law governing secured-transaction remedies on commercial collateral.
  • Special assets — non-performing or special-situations loans needing tighter monitoring and faster action.

FAQ

Is ACH the same as NACHA?

No. ACH is the payment network; NACHA publishes the operating rules that govern ACH transactions. A company follows NACHA guidelines but is not 'NACHA certified'.

What is the difference between servicing and sub-servicing?

Servicing is administering the loan; sub-servicing is doing that on behalf of a lender who owns the loans but outsources the operation.
