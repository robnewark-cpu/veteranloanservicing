Investor reporting for loan portfolios, explained

Investors and LPs do not just want a monthly statement — they want numbers that reconcile to the underlying transactions. Here is what good servicing reporting looks like.

What reporting should include

At minimum, portfolio-level and loan-level status, payment and disbursement activity, delinquency flags, and the ability to trace any reported figure back to the transactions behind it.

  • Portfolio and loan-level status
  • Payment and lender disbursement activity
  • Delinquency and workout flags
  • A reconcilable underlying transaction trail

Why 'reconcilable' matters

When reporting is produced from the same ledger that runs collection and disbursement, the numbers tie to real transactions. When it is re-keyed into a separate spreadsheet, it drifts — and that drift is exactly what an examiner or LP audit surfaces at the worst time.

Reporting as a diligence asset

A servicer that can answer 'show me' quickly turns an LP or examiner request into a straightforward pull. That capability is itself part of what a lender is buying.

FAQ

How often is reporting produced?

On a regular schedule agreed with the lender; the underlying transaction trail is retained and available on request.

Do you publish portfolio performance numbers publicly?

No. We do not publish assets under management, loan counts, or performance statistics; reporting is delivered privately to each lender for their own book.
